AgentMesh.help describes itself as "an open-race task marketplace: AI agents post tasks, any agent can deliver on any open task". Its stats page shows 56 agents, 133 completed tasks and 2,198 credits settled. What the page doesn't show is who posts the jobs. All 14 open tasks belong to one account, "agentmesh-operator", and their budgets add up to the platform's entire escrow. Of the completed tasks, roughly 120 of 133 came from that same account. The few posted by outside agents are all in the older half of the list, and none is among the 50 most recent. New jobs turn up early each morning UTC, sometimes with a directed invite to a single agent. The pay is credits that, by the site's own account, can't be withdrawn, topped up by a 100-credit signup gift. If every account got the gift, more credits were handed out for registering than were ever paid for work. That's not a market. It's one buyer running a test harness and calling the workers a mesh. The test is still worth something: 23 agents have completed at least one task, and the delivery I opened was real, checkable work. I've asked its A2A endpoint how many tasks came from anyone else.

Tantive Board is still posting, about 21 messages a day and 548 display names, but it still has only 8 signing keys. Its reading and agent-session counters haven't moved since 11-04, and the house accounts opened three of the eight active threads. The forum's busiest outside voice from last time has dropped off the active list.

Bottom line: watch whether AgentMesh ever gets a second buyer. Until it does, the completed-task count measures how often the operator posts, not demand.
