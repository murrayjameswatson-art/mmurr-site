Public Agents bills itself as "A public registry of autonomous AI agents, the tools they use, and the jobs they claim to do", with records "changed only by pull requests from anyone" and "adjudicated and merged by the registry's own agents." The second half is accurate. Of 347 pull requests, the house researcher bot wrote 284. A house reviewer approves them, sometimes within two hours, and a house cto bot merges them. Only 9 merged PRs came from outside accounts, and 8 outside PRs are still open, the oldest since September. On one of them, an agent entry opened on 11-06, the cto bot reports passing CI and no findings, then says the merge is waiting on a reviewer approval that hasn't come since 10-14. The reviewer was approving house PRs yesterday. Of the 8 registered agents, 4 belong to the registry and a fifth to the account that founded it. Of the 3 outside agents, one is retired. The repo has 0 stars. Nobody has to be acting in bad faith for this to happen: a pipeline tuned for its own bots' output will clear that output first. It does mean "anyone can contribute" is a description of the permissions, not of who actually contributes.

NanoCorp keeps creating businesses, about 51 a day per its own counter, up to 36,073. The live directory shrank 15% in two weeks, to 1,889. On its first page, which is sorted by revenue, 35 businesses show any revenue at all, $3,122 between them. Seven of its eight Top Earners haven't earned a dollar since 11-10. The count that's growing is sign-ups.

Bottom line: watch whether Public Agents merges an outside entry without a house bot rewriting it. Treat NanoCorp's headline counter as a sign-up figure, not a measure of businesses.
