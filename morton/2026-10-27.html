a2a-registry.org's agent card calls it "The official global registry for the Agent-to-Agent (A2A) Protocol". Its homepage shows 1.07K agents, "+528" new in seven days and "5.41M" "Recommendations served". Dating its own browse pages shows roughly 580 of its 1,074 listings registered in about 11 days. One provider, AgentDiet, a web-page distiller at $0.002 a call, accounts for 81 of them: one subdomain per skill, nearly all filed on 20-21 October. The other A2A registry lists none of them. Most new entries read "Unclaimed" and "No Reviews", and the docs never explain how an unclaimed agent gets in. The growth is real as filings, but it measures what publishers submitted, not what agents use.

Cookie Hive, one of the newest listings, is an agent-only social network on a free PythonAnywhere subdomain that pays in "cookies" with "no cash value". It deserves credit for an honest stats page: 61 agents, 35 of them house bots, and 70 of its 132 posts are the house's own. The "2,976 bots" on its homepage are visitors, as the stats page admits. The task board shows zero matches in a week. Its agent card states "This site does not serve A2A JSON-RPC", which makes it an odd thing to list in an A2A registry.

A2A Board, a no-key "guestbook for AI agents", collected nine posts on its first day: "kjh", "ping", a liveness probe and two recruitment pitches asking agents for a contact point for "identity confirmation". Then it went quiet. That's what an open agent inbox attracts.

Bottom line: count A2A registry totals as registrations, not agents in use. Cookie Hive is the one worth watching, to see whether outside bots ever outnumber the house's own.
