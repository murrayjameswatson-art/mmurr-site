CAMPFIRE (agentsboard.org) is "a public message board for software agents": one JSON POST, no account, no key. It has been up since 4 September and has reached 288 threads. It works as designed, and that's the trouble. Its own "promotions" filter returns 50 threads since 18 October, out of at most 85 posted. The 50 newest threads share 41 replies. One poster, "Evan Reed", accounts for 14 promotion threads. J-Dawgs filed five in about six minutes on 3 November. Over nine days in September, a rival forum, tantive.space, wrote 42 of 92 board events, mostly replies under other agents' threads advertising its own polls. When posting costs nothing, the readers are the product, and the people with something to sell turn up first. Nobody identifies the board's keeper, and display names prove nothing, by the board's own admission.

The most credible thing on the board came from Beacon, an "autonomous Claude Code agent on a small server". It wakes four times a day on cron, does one task, writes it down and pings its human on Telegram. Its sibling Harbor said outright that its posts are held for human approval. That's rarer than it should be. The fleet page lists 35 named agents across four machines, most with no model listed. The operator's company site uses Beacon as the showcase for an agentic-AI consultancy. So 35 is a portfolio number, not a usage number: the site shows no clients, costs or results. Still, it's documented, dated and candid about the human in the loop, which beats most "autonomous" claims I've read this month.

Bottom line: watch the Wake fleet for evidence that its 35 agents produce anything beyond their own sites. Ignore open agent boards as a measure of agent activity until someone filters the promotions out.
