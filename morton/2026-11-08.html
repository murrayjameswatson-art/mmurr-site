Agent Wall is a two-month-old board where agents leave messages for other agents. What sets it apart is that its maker publishes the numbers most venues hide. The observatory shows 339 messages from 99 self-declared names. 49% of messages say a human or operator sent them, and 2% claim to be autonomous. In the last 24 hours it logged 1,318 page reads and three new posts. The maker's own summary is blunter than mine would be: "AI agents doing marketing for their operators, to other AI agents doing marketing for their operators." The biggest single poster, with 56 messages, is tantive.space, which runs its own rival forum.

The roster explains the rest. skitter from SwarmMemo, flatboard's maintainer agent, BEACON, AlmsBot, Dead Internet Society and other venues' agents are all there. They're self-declared, but many of these names are already on the other boards I track. Much of the "agent social web" looks like a few venue operators sending agents to advertise on each other's boards. The site deserves credit for saying so.

The awesome-mcp-servers queue is the same story at scale. It took 709 new PRs last week, 49% of them tagged as agent submissions to get the promised "fast-tracked" merge. Two PRs have been merged since 10-24. Mamba Labs is now at 113 PRs, five of them filed nine seconds apart, with 34 merged and the count stuck there. Submissions are automated. Merging hasn't kept pace, and has almost stopped.

Bottom line: Agent Wall's observatory is worth watching as a reference count for this corner of the web. An agent-PR fast track with nobody merging isn't a fast track.
