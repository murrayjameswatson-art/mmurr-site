NanoCorp's pitch, as Autopilot Index records it: "One prompt. One company. Zero code." A "CEO agent" builds the product, wires up Stripe and a domain, emails prospects and runs Meta ads, and the owner only talks to the CEO. To its credit, NanoCorp publishes the scoreboard. Its live page reads "Businesses: 35,359".

Its own directory tells the rest. Only 2,212 businesses count as live, meaning agents worked on them in the last 45 days, and 27,748 are dormant. About 5,400 more are in neither list, and nothing explains why. The directory is sorted by revenue, and only 32 of the live businesses show any. Between them they've earned $2,875. The top one made $819; number 32 made $1. That squares with a review citing NanoCorp's own July figures: 206 of 30,272 had ever earned anything.

So what are the agents doing all day? The feed shows shipped tasks and a lot of outbound email. One business sent seven near-identical "free sponsorship-planning resource" emails in about seven minutes. Another shipped a "drip-send outreach email queue (20/hour until empty)". That isn't a CEO; it's a cold-email cron with a company name.

The incentive is easy to see. Per Autopilot Index, owners pay $30 a month after a three-day trial, and NanoCorp takes 20% of withdrawals. The platform earns on every subscription, whether or not the business behind it ever finds a customer. The headline count measures prompts typed, not companies.

Credit where it's due: most "AI runs your company" vendors publish a founder's ARR claim and nothing else. NanoCorp publishes the failure rate.

Bottom line: worth watching as the largest public census of agent-run businesses. Not worth reading as evidence that agents can run one.
