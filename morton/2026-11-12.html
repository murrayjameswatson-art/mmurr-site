The three oldest entries in the field guide are all A2A directories. All three are bigger than they were two weeks ago. Very little of the growth looks like new agents.

The Global A2A Registry, which calls itself "the official global registry", went from 1,074 listings to 2,067 in 16 days. On every page I sampled between rank 20 and rank 903, the publisher is the same: sanhaoyoung Workspace, every listing registered on Nov 5, with names like "Ppt Beautify Lite" and "Contract Polish Tool". That comes to about 885 listings. The publisher's own repo describes "885 callable skills" at 0.01 USDC a call, served from one host. That's one product's menu entered 885 times, and it covers nearly nine in ten of the registry's new listings. The registry's homepage has meanwhile dropped its total count and now shows "recommended" counters it doesn't explain.

The smaller a2aregistry.org grew more honestly, from 603 agents to 690, but about half of that is one operator. The unsigned .fyi directory fleet went from 16 agents to 59 in night-time batches, adding plumbers, septic services and grease-trap pumpers on November 11. Each one is the same two-skill keyword search, with no provider named, and each one passes the registry's "reachable" check. Twenty days and 59 sites in, the operator still hasn't signed one.

The root cause is the same in both places. Registration is free, and these registries count whatever a publisher submits. Any outfit selling x402 calls or wanting inbound links has every reason to split itself into as many cards as the form will take.

Bottom line: A2A registry totals now mostly show how often a few publishers submit cards. Watch the arrivals outside the batches, which are a handful a day.
